# Food and culture

## Research

Guidebooks have strong food and culture chapters. Search for:

- "eat & drink like a local", "local specialities", "food", "cuisine", plus the region,
- the specific dish or drink if the user named one,
- "eating" plus the neighbourhood or town for restaurant listings (with price bands),
- "etiquette", "tipping", "dos & don'ts", "religion", "festivals", "month by month".

Read the best pages before answering, and cite them.

## Food answers

- **Dishes to try:** 4–8 items, each with a one-line description of what it is, and where or
  when to have it (region, season, time of day).
- **Where to eat:** a few named places from the guidebook with neighbourhood and price band,
  cited by page, plus a generic tip (e.g. "lunch set menus are much cheaper than dinner").
  Mark them as possibly closed or changed since publication.
- **Dietary needs:** if the user mentioned vegetarian, vegan, allergies or halal/kosher, call
  out which local dishes work, which phrases help, and common hidden ingredients (e.g. fish
  sauce, lard, anchovies).

## Culture answers

Short, practical and respectful:

- **Etiquette:** greetings, shoes off, how to behave at temples, churches or sacred sites.
- **Dress codes:** covered shoulders/knees where required.
- **Tipping and paying:** customary amounts, service charges, cash vs card.
- **Taboos to avoid**, framed as helpful tips rather than stereotypes.
- **Festivals or events** during the user's dates, if known.

If the user is building an itinerary, suggest where meals or cultural experiences fit into
specific days.
